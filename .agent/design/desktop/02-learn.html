substantive

adjective formal

Having real importance, value or effect; about the actual content of something, not just its form.

বাংলাসারগর্ভ, মৌলিক, বাস্তব গুরুত্বপূর্ণ

How it's used

Common in academic, legal and professional writing to say something is real and meaningful, not surface-level or procedural. In everyday speech people usually say “real” or “meaningful” instead.

Collocations
substantive changes substantive issue substantive discussion substantive feedback substantive evidence
Patterns
  • make substantive progress on sth
  • have a substantive discussion about sth
  • no substantive difference between A and B
Examples
  1. The reviewer gave substantive feedback on my methodology, not just grammar fixes.
  2. We need a substantive discussion about the budget before we sign the contract.
  3. After three meetings, there has been no substantive progress on the project.
  4. Version 2 includes substantive changes to the API, so read the release notes.
Don't confuse

substantial — large in size, amount or importance: “a substantial increase in cost”.

is it formal?
Reply · substantive

Yes, it's formal. You'll see it in research papers, reports, legal text and serious work emails.

In casual conversation it can sound stiff. Native speakers usually say “real”, “meaningful” or “important”: “We didn't make any real progress.”
